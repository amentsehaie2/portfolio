# The €150 Home Studio: Essential Recording Gear for Guitarists

*Recording guitar at home doesn't have to cost a fortune. My core setup cost about €150, and it taught me more through its problems than it ever did when it worked.*

![My first two electric guitars, a dark blue one and a white Squier Stratocaster, leaning against a desk with a laptop, MIDI keyboard and headphones](/images/blog/first-setup.webp "portrait")
*Figure 1. My first home setup. Photo by the author.*

## I didn't buy it all at once

When people see a "home studio" online, they picture racks of gear. Mine came together one piece at a time, over almost a year:

1. **Right away:** a guitar, an amp and a cable.
2. **About six months later:** my first audio interface.
3. **About two months after that:** headphones I could actually record with.
4. **Already had it:** a basic HP laptop with only 4 GB of RAM.

That slow build is something I'd recommend. You find out what you actually need by using what you have.

The guitar, interface and cable are what you actually need to record. That part cost me around €150. The amp was nice to have, but as you'll see below, I barely needed it for recording.

## What went wrong (and what it taught me)

### 1. The cheap cable that slowly died

I bought the cheapest cable my local shop had. One day, while making music, a scratching noise started coming through my headphones. I had no idea what it was, so I ignored it. Then my guitar stopped making any sound at all.

After a lot of plugging in and out, I found that the cable only worked if I held it in one specific position. The cable was broken inside.

**Lesson:** the cable is the one place not to save money. I replaced it with Fender cables (around €17 each) and haven't had that problem since. The cheap Devine cable lasted around a year, which I don't recommend. I'd tell anyone to buy a Fender cable instead, which can last up to 8 years with proper care.

### 2. Hearing myself late (latency)

When I played, the sound in my headphones arrived slightly *after* I hit the strings. That delay is called **latency**, and it makes playing in time really hard.

At the time I blamed my old secondhand interface. Looking back, a big part of it is how your computer handles audio. The **buffer size** is how much audio the computer collects before processing it. A bigger buffer is more stable but adds delay; a smaller one is faster but harder on your computer ([Focusrite explains this clearly](https://support.focusrite.com/hc/en-gb/articles/207546885)). With only 4 GB of RAM, my laptop struggled. Cakewalk froze on me several times, and every time I was scared I'd lost my whole project.

**Lesson:** before buying new gear, try a lower buffer size and close other programs. And save often.

### 3. The hum I couldn't explain

When I stopped playing, there was an annoying background noise in my recordings. Two things were going on.

First, **single-coil pickups hum.** They act a bit like an antenna and pick up interference from the electricity around you. In Europe that's a 50 Hz hum, in the US 60 Hz ([how pickups pick up mains hum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pickup_(music_technology))). I noticed it was there on my neck pickup (a single coil) but disappeared when I switched to the bridge humbucker, which is built to cancel that hum.

Second, I didn't know **noise gates** existed. After reading how professional guitarists talked about them, I added one in my plugin chain in Cakewalk. A gate mutes the signal when you're not playing, so the noise between phrases just disappeared. Once I understood how to set it, my recordings got noticeably cleaner. *(More on getting clean guitar tracks in [INTERNAL LINK: Post 05, "Electric vs. Acoustic"].)*

## The free software that changed my tone

My Mooer amp had effects built in, but it never gave me the sound I wanted. The interface changed that. Instead of recording the amp, I plugged the guitar straight into the interface and used free amp simulators in Cakewalk.

The one that impressed me most was [TONE3000](https://www.tone3000.com), a free library of amp captures. People record ("capture") real amps and pedals with Neural Amp Modeler technology and share them, so you can play through a realistic version of an amp you could never afford. I load the captures with the NAM plugin.

That's when I realized that for recording at home, **the amp was optional.** The software did more than my amp ever could.

## What I use now

My setup has grown, but slowly:

- **Focusrite Scarlett 2i2** as my interface. I chose it because it's the mainstream choice and known for reliability.
- **Fender cables**For the reasons above.
- **A navy-blue Stagg Stratocaster** With all single-coil pickups, which I got for free. 
- **Valeton GP-100 multi-effects pedal**Bought about two years after I started. Not for home recording, but because I needed a better sound in church. That story is in [INTERNAL LINK: Post 04, "First Time Playing at the Sanctuary"].
- **HP Pavilion x360 laptop**The touchscreen is handy for moving faders and clicking around in the DAW.


## My advice if you have about €150

Here's what I'd tell a beginner today:

1. **Buy a guitar and an audio interface first.** A Focusrite is a safe pick.
2. **Spend a little more on a good cable.** It's cheap insurance.
3. **Skip the amp if you only want to record at home.** Plug straight into the interface and use free amp sims.
4. **Buy secondhand.** My interface cost €20 on Marktplaats. New gear costs more, so with today's prices a new setup may land above €150. 
5. **If you want to expand later,** get a proper amp and a multi-effects pedal.

But the most important thing isn't gear at all: **learn the philosophy behind tone.** If you understand *why* a sound works, you can rebuild it anywhere, on a laptop, on a pedal or on a church PA.

## Key takeaways

- A guitar, an interface and a decent cable are all you need to start recording.
- Most "my gear is bad" problems are really settings: buffer size, pickup choice and a noise gate.
- Free tools like Cakewalk and TONE3000 can replace an amp at home.
- Build slowly and upgrade only what actually holds you back.
